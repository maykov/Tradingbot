# Seed Briefs — 10 Candidate Ideas (Target: $200M ARR within 5 years)

Generated 2026-07-02 by the Orchestrator (Phase 1). Each brief: problem, customer, product in one
sentence, why now, first-guess revenue model. All market-size claims below are orchestrator
assumptions to be validated by the Entrepreneur agents.

---

## Idea 01 — SentinelAML (fintech / regtech)
**Problem:** Regional banks, credit unions, and fintechs run AML/transaction monitoring on legacy
rules engines that generate 90%+ false positives, forcing them to staff armies of analysts and
still eat rising enforcement fines. **Customer:** Compliance teams at mid-size banks, credit
unions, fintechs, and crypto exchanges. **Product:** An AI-native transaction-monitoring and
case-management platform that cuts false positives, explains every alert, and auto-drafts SAR
narratives. **Why now:** LLMs make alert triage and narrative generation viable; regulators are
signaling acceptance of model-based monitoring; AML fines and personal liability for compliance
officers keep climbing. **Revenue model:** SaaS subscription tiered on monitored transaction
volume, ~$150k–$1M ACV. **TAM guess:** financial-crime compliance software + analyst labor
displacement, >$15B.

## Idea 02 — ClearPath Health (healthtech)
**Problem:** Prior authorization costs US providers tens of billions per year in administrative
burden, denials, and delayed care. **Customer:** Hospital systems, specialty clinics, and MSOs.
**Product:** AI agents that assemble clinical evidence from the EHR, submit prior-auth requests
through payer APIs and portals, and manage appeals end-to-end. **Why now:** CMS interoperability
and electronic prior-auth mandates (2026–2027) force payers to expose APIs; LLMs can read charts
and payer policies. **Revenue model:** Per-transaction (~$2–8/auth) plus platform subscription.
**TAM guess:** US prior-auth admin spend >$30B/yr.

## Idea 03 — InferRoute (AI infrastructure)
**Problem:** Enterprises running workloads across multiple LLM providers overspend 40–70% on
inference and lack cost governance, failover, and policy control. **Customer:** Platform and AI
engineering teams at mid-to-large enterprises. **Product:** An inference gateway providing smart
model routing, semantic caching, failover, spend analytics, and policy enforcement across
providers. **Why now:** Model proliferation plus CFO scrutiny of exploding AI budgets. **Revenue
model:** Usage-based fee (% of managed inference spend or per-token) plus enterprise platform fee.
**TAM guess:** enterprise LLM inference spend headed past $100B/yr; gateway layer captures 2–5%.

## Idea 04 — ClearBorder (logistics / trade)
**Problem:** Mid-market importers and e-commerce brands face volatile tariffs, complex HS
classification, and a fragmented, manual customs-brokerage industry. **Customer:** Mid-market
importers/exporters, 3PLs, cross-border e-commerce brands. **Product:** An AI customs brokerage —
automated HS classification, duty optimization, filing, and landed-cost prediction. **Why now:**
Tariff turbulence of 2025–26, de-minimis rule changes, and digitization of customs regimes
worldwide. **Revenue model:** Per-filing fees plus SaaS for trade-compliance teams. **TAM guess:**
global customs brokerage ~$25B plus trade-compliance software ~$10B.

## Idea 05 — GridPilot (climate / energy)
**Problem:** Commercial buildings, battery owners, and EV fleets hold gigawatts of flexible
capacity they cannot monetize in energy markets. **Customer:** C&I property owners, fleet
operators, and battery installers, with utilities as a channel. **Product:** Virtual power plant
(VPP) software that aggregates distributed batteries, EVs, and HVAC into wholesale market bids and
demand-response programs. **Why now:** FERC Order 2222 opens wholesale markets to aggregated DERs;
battery costs keep falling; AI-datacenter load growth is straining grids and raising the value of
flexibility. **Revenue model:** 20–30% share of market revenue generated plus SaaS. **TAM guess:**
US grid-flexibility value pool >$20B/yr by 2030.

## Idea 06 — AgentLens (developer tools)
**Problem:** Companies deploying AI agents in production cannot see failures, regressions, cost
blowouts, or unsafe actions until customers complain. **Customer:** AI engineering teams shipping
agentic products. **Product:** Observability, evaluation, and guardrails for AI agents — traces,
replay, continuous evals, safety monitors, and incident alerting. **Why now:** Agent deployments
are exploding ahead of any standard tooling; agent incidents are expensive and public. **Revenue
model:** Usage-based (traces/evals) plus seats and an enterprise tier. **TAM guess:** shadow of
the observability market ($20B+) as agents become a first-class workload.

## Idea 07 — CollectFlow (B2B SaaS / fintech)
**Problem:** Trillions of dollars sit in late B2B receivables; SMB and mid-market AR teams are
understaffed and dunning is awkward, manual, and inconsistent. **Customer:** CFOs and AR teams at
SMB/mid-market firms; embedded distribution via accounting platforms. **Product:** An autonomous
AR agent that personalizes dunning, negotiates payment plans within policy, reconciles payments,
and escalates cleanly. **Why now:** LLMs can conduct personalized, polite negotiation at scale;
elevated interest rates make working capital precious. **Revenue model:** SaaS plus a percentage
of accelerated collections; white-label deals with accounting platforms. **TAM guess:** AR
automation + collections outsourcing >$15B.

## Idea 08 — RoboCrew (robotics / marketplace)
**Problem:** Mid-size warehouses need automation but cannot afford robotics capex, integration
projects, or vendor evaluation. **Customer:** 3PLs and mid-market warehouse operators.
**Product:** A robotics-as-a-service marketplace bundling vetted robots (AMRs, picking arms),
financing, integration, and a unified orchestration layer, billed per task. **Why now:**
Persistent warehouse labor shortage; robot hardware commoditizing; RaaS financing structures
maturing. **Revenue model:** Per-pick/per-hour usage fees with OEM revenue share, plus
orchestration SaaS. **TAM guess:** warehouse automation services >$30B by 2030.

## Idea 09 — FactoryEye (industrial AI)
**Problem:** Mid-market manufacturers lose 2–4% of revenue to quality escapes and scrap but cannot
hire ML teams or fund six-month machine-vision integrations. **Customer:** Quality directors at
mid-market discrete manufacturers. **Product:** Turnkey AI visual inspection — edge cameras plus
foundation vision models — deployable on a line in days with no ML expertise required. **Why
now:** Vision foundation models slash per-line training cost; reshoring boom; chronic QA labor
shortage. **Revenue model:** Per-line subscription (hardware + software), ~$30–60k/line/yr.
**TAM guess:** machine-vision inspection market >$15B and underpenetrated in the mid-market.

## Idea 10 — KinCare (consumer / care economy)
**Problem:** ~53M US family caregivers coordinate eldercare through spreadsheets, group texts, and
phone tag across a fragmented provider and benefits landscape. **Customer:** Working family
caregivers, monetized through employers (as a benefit) and Medicare Advantage plans. **Product:**
An AI care-coordination platform — care plans, provider and benefits navigation, medication and
appointment orchestration, family task-sharing, and a vetted service marketplace. **Why now:**
Aging demographics, an employer caregiver-benefits arms race, MA supplemental benefits, and AI
finally making 1:many care navigation economical. **Revenue model:** B2B2C PEPM from employers and
health plans plus marketplace take rate. **TAM guess:** US eldercare coordination and navigation
>$20B.
