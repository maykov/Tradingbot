# 03 — PRODUCTION DESIGN

Film: **THE BET**. All names and locations match `02_script.md` exactly. Period: Imperial Russia, 1885–1900.

---

# SECTION A — COSTUME DESIGN

## PYOTR SEMYONOVICH VOLKOV (the Banker)

### Scene 1 — The Wager (1885, age 42)
- **Wardrobe:** Black evening tailcoat of fine wool broadcloth, satin lapels, perfectly fitted across broad shoulders. White piqué waistcoat, double-breasted, with three gold buttons. Stiff white wing-collar shirt, white piqué bow tie. Black wool trousers with satin side stripe. All garments new, pressed, gleaming.
- **Footwear:** Black patent-leather pumps, mirror shine.
- **Accessories:** Heavy gold signet ring on right little finger (worn in every scene — continuity anchor). Gold pocket-watch chain in double swag across the waistcoat. A lit Havana cigar, 15 cm, in the right hand for most of the scene.
- **Hair/Makeup:** Thick dark-brown hair pomaded straight back with a high shine. Full dark-brown beard trimmed square at 6 cm. Ruddy, well-fed complexion; light sheen of party warmth on forehead and cheeks.

### Scene 4 — The Hunger for Everything, garden portion (1891, age 48)
- **Wardrobe:** Dark-gray double-breasted wool overcoat with black velvet collar, worn open over a charcoal frock coat and dove-gray waistcoat. White shirt, dark-blue four-in-hand tie. Black trousers.
- **Footwear:** Black leather ankle boots, lightly mud-dusted at the soles.
- **Accessories:** Same gold signet ring. Black silk top hat held in left hand; the six-language letter in right hand.
- **Hair/Makeup:** Hair now half-gray, still pomaded but thinner at the crown. Beard iron-and-brown, square but less crisp. Complexion duller; first pouches under the eyes.
- **Narrative meaning of change:** Six years of financial strain visible — the costume is still expensive but a season out of fashion.

### Scenes 5, 6, 7, 8 — The Murder Night (November 13–14, 1900, age 57)
- **Wardrobe (Scene 5, study):** Rust-brown quilted dressing gown of worn silk, frayed at the cuffs, over a collarless white shirt yellowed at the neck, and black trousers shiny with wear. For Scenes 6–7 he adds: a heavy black wool overcoat with astrakhan collar (the astrakhan matted and balding in patches), collar turned up.
- **Footwear:** Scene 5: brown leather slippers. Scenes 6–8: black galoshes over ankle boots, mud-caked and wet from the garden.
- **Accessories:** Gold signet ring, now visibly loose, spinning on the thinned finger. Brass hurricane lamp (Scenes 6–7). Iron lodge key. In Scene 8 the coat stays on, rain-dark across the shoulders.
- **Hair/Makeup:** Hair thin, white at temples, uncombed. Beard fully iron-gray, untrimmed, 8 cm. Skin sallow-gray, deep pouches under the eyes, rain on the face in Scenes 6–7; in Scene 7 add tear tracks through the rain. Hands with a visible fine tremor.
- **Narrative meaning of change:** The 1885 magnate has rotted inside his own clothes; the ring that fit a fat finger now spins on a thin one.

### Scene 9 — Five Hours Early (morning, 1900)
- **Wardrobe:** Same black astrakhan-collar overcoat, buttoned crooked (one button off) — he dressed without a valet, without sleeping. Beneath: the same yellowed shirt from the night.
- **Footwear:** Same wet galoshes over boots.
- **Accessories:** Signet ring; the folded letter, carried inside the coat over the chest from mid-scene.
- **Hair/Makeup:** As Scenes 5–8 but drier; eyes red-rimmed from the sleepless night; gray morning pallor.

## NIKOLAI ANDREYEVICH MOROZOV (the Lawyer)

### Scene 1 — The Wager (1885, age 25)
- **Wardrobe:** Black frock coat of cheap but well-brushed wool, slightly shiny at the elbows, fitted close to a slender frame. Black single-breasted waistcoat, white shirt with a soft turndown collar, narrow black cravat. Black trousers, hems lightly worn.
- **Footwear:** Black leather ankle boots, polished but creased and resoled.
- **Accessories:** None — pointedly, the only man in the room without watch chain or rings. Signs the wager with Orlov's pen.
- **Hair/Makeup:** Black hair, clean, slightly long, pushed behind the ears. Clean-shaven. Pale, smooth skin; feverish flush high on the cheekbones during the argument; dark-brown eyes accented to read as "burning."

### Scene 2 — Year One (1886, age 26)
- **Wardrobe:** Same white shirt (collar open, no cravat) and black waistcoat, unbuttoned. The frock coat from Scene 1 hangs on a wall nail throughout the scene — a visible relic of the outside world. Black trousers, gray wool socks.
- **Footwear:** Soft felt house shoes.
- **Accessories:** None.
- **Hair/Makeup:** Hair to the collar now, still black, combed. Clean-shaven but by his own hand — one small nick on the jaw. Prison pallor beginning: reduce the cheek flush to zero.

### Scene 3 — Year Five (1890, age 30)
- **Wardrobe:** Wine-stained dressing gown of faded bottle-green wool over a gray undershirt. Black trousers, no belt. The frock coat still on its nail, now dusty.
- **Footwear:** Barefoot on the bed; felt shoes abandoned mid-floor.
- **Accessories:** Wine glass; bottle within arm's reach on the floor.
- **Hair/Makeup:** Hair to the shoulders, unwashed, pushed back with fingers. Beard four months old, patchy, dark. Face puffy, eyes bloodshot, lips wine-darkened. Fingernails long. Overall tone: sallow ivory.

### Scene 4 — The Hunger for Everything (1891–94, ages 31–34)
- **Wardrobe:** Clean collarless white shirt, sleeves rolled to the elbow; dark waistcoat buttoned; black trousers. Everything worn but scrupulously neat — the scholar's discipline. In the 1894 Gospel section: add a shapeless gray knitted cardigan against the winter cold.
- **Footwear:** Felt shoes, then felt boots (valenki) in the winter section.
- **Accessories:** Steel-nibbed pen; ink-stained right middle finger (keep this stain for the rest of the film).
- **Hair/Makeup:** Hair long, tied back with a bootlace at the nape. Beard now full, trimmed once with scissors, first gray threads. Gaunt: cheekbones prominent, temples hollow, but eyes clear and alive — the healthiest he looks after Scene 1.
- **Narrative meaning of change:** Order returns to the body when purpose returns to the mind.

### Scenes 7 and 9 — The Letter / the empty room (1900, age 40)
- **Wardrobe (Scene 7, asleep at the table):** A long black coat — the Scene 1 frock coat itself, taken down from the nail at last, now moth-eaten at the shoulders, hanging on a skeletal frame like a scarecrow's coat. Beneath it the gray knitted cardigan and a colorless collarless shirt. Black trousers gone gray at the knees.
- **Footwear:** Cracked leather boots, laced with string.
- **Accessories:** None. The letter lies under his crossed arms.
- **Hair/Makeup:** THE key makeup build of the film. Long gray-streaked curls past the shoulders; shaggy gray-brown beard to mid-chest. Skin yellow-toned and earthy, drawn tight over the skull; cheeks hollow, eye sockets deep, lips thin and bloodless. The narrow hand on the table: long thin fingers, prominent tendons, the old ink stain. He must read as a man of sixty who is forty.
- **Narrative meaning of change:** He leaves the lodge wearing the coat he arrived in — the bet has consumed the body inside it but returned the same soul, refined.
- **Scene 9:** Morozov does not appear; only the costume's absence — the empty chair, the nail on the wall finally bare.

## STEPAN (the Watchman)

### All scenes (2, 3, 4, 6, 8 O.S., 9)
- **Wardrobe:** Long watchman's coat of coarse gray wool (armyak), rope-belted, hem mud-fringed; beneath it a red-brown peasant shirt and gray homespun trousers. In winter scenes add a sheepskin vest, fleece inward. In Scene 6 he sleeps under the full sheepskin coat.
- **Footwear:** Gray felt boots (valenki) in winter scenes; birch-bark-and-leather boots in the autumn scenes; in Scene 9, felt boots soaked and mud-caked to the knee.
- **Accessories:** Brass-handled watchman's rattle at the belt; ring of three iron keys; in Scene 4 the double-barreled shotgun.
- **Hair/Makeup:** White hair cut round in the peasant style (pudding-bowl), round white beard. Weather-browned face and hands; milky-blue eyes; enormous knotted knuckles. Age him subtly from Scene 2 (early 50s) to Scene 9 (66) by whitening the beard fully and deepening the stoop, not by prosthetics.
- **Costume changes and meaning:** Stepan never changes style, only weathers — he is the estate's one constant, marking time by decay of the same garments.

## YEVGENY IVANOVICH ORLOV (the Journalist)

### Scene 1 only (1885, age 50)
- **Wardrobe:** Dove-gray evening tailcoat (deliberately unusual against the other guests' black — he must be findable in the wide shots), pale-gray silk waistcoat, white shirt, gray bow tie, black trousers.
- **Footwear:** Black patent pumps.
- **Accessories:** Gold pince-nez on a black silk ribbon pinned to the lapel; slim silver pencil and a small red notebook (he writes the terms of the bet in it); brandy balloon.
- **Hair/Makeup:** Fine silver hair brushed to the right, pointed silver goatee, pale intelligent face, ironic single raised eyebrow as the default expression.

## PARTY GUESTS (Scene 1, background, 6 persons)
- Four men: black evening tailcoats, white shirts, black or white bow ties, one in a dark-blue dress uniform with silver buttons (a retired officer). Two women: high-necked evening gowns in garnet-red silk and pale-gold satin respectively, hair in high chignons, small jet and pearl jewelry. All costumes read as 1885 Moscow money: heavy fabrics, gaslight-warm colors.

# SECTION B — SET DESIGN

## LOCATION 1 — VOLKOV MANSION, DRAWING ROOM (Scene 1)

- **Architecture and dimensions:** Rectangular room, 12 m long × 7 m wide × 4.5 m ceiling, in a neoclassical country mansion. Long axis runs from double entrance doors (south wall) to a white Carrara-marble fireplace (north wall). Three tall French windows on the west wall, each 3 m high, giving onto the black night garden; the middle window sightlines directly to the distant GARDEN LODGE's lit window (compositing anchor for the scene's final shot).
- **Color palette:** Deep viridian-green damask wallpaper with a vertical self-stripe; white-painted paneling and cornices; gilt picture frames; crimson and amber accents from upholstery and candlelight. Overall: warm gold light on dark green — wealth at night.
- **Floor:** Herringbone oak parquet, honey-toned, high polish; a 5 × 3.5 m Persian carpet (crimson field, indigo border) centered between fireplace and seating.
- **Ceiling:** White plaster with a central acanthus rose; from it hangs a two-tier crystal chandelier holding 24 lit candles.
- **Furniture and placement:** At the north end before the fireplace, a conversation group: a crimson-velvet three-seat sofa facing the fire at 3 m, flanked by two gilt bergère chairs in cream-and-crimson stripe (Morozov sits in the east-side bergère). Between them a low mahogany table with brandy decanter and six balloons. Along the east wall: two card tables in green baize, abandoned mid-game — scattered cards, ivory counters, full ashtrays. Between the west windows: a mahogany console with a silver candelabrum (5 candles) and a bronze mantel clock reading 11:40. Grand piano (rosewood, closed) in the southwest corner. Potted palms (two, 2 m) in brass jardinières flanking the entrance doors.
- **Walls:** Gilt-framed oil portraits — largest, over the fireplace, a 1.5 m portrait of Volkov painted that year: ruddy, confident, arms crossed (this exact portrait recurs in the Study set in 1900).
- **Windows and light sources:** Candlelight only — chandelier, candelabrum, fireplace flame, plus two oil lamps with etched-glass globes on side tables. Color temperature ~1900 K, warm amber. The windows read as cold blue-black rectangles (moonless night), with the lodge's single warm pixel of light far off in the middle window.
- **Practical elements characters interact with:** The fireplace (Volkov's station — he flicks cigar ash into it); the brandy table; the bell-pull beside the fireplace; Orlov's red notebook on the low table where the terms are signed; the middle French window (final shot).
- **Ambient mood:** Overheated, smoky, glittering; the air visibly hazed with cigar smoke in backlight.
- **Time of day:** 11:30 p.m.–midnight, November 14, 1885.

## LOCATION 2 — GARDEN LODGE, MAIN ROOM (Scenes 2, 3, 4, 7, 9)

One set, four dressed states. The bones never change — only the dressing.

- **Architecture and dimensions:** A single-story brick lodge, whitewashed inside, one main room 8 m × 5 m × 2.8 m to exposed dark-stained beams. South wall: the sealed entrance DOOR (heavy oak, iron lock) and beside it, at 1.5 m height, the hinged WINDOW PANEL — an oak-framed hatch 40 cm × 30 cm opening outward. West wall: one casement WINDOW, 90 cm × 120 cm, four panes, deep sill 30 cm, hinged inward, at 1 m height — big enough for a thin man to climb through (Scene 9 requires this). North wall: iron wood-stove with a black flue pipe, and the narrow iron-framed BED (mattress, gray wool blankets). East wall: the upright PIANO (black, German, candle sconces on the front board). Center-west, under the window's light: the long oak TABLE (2.2 m × 0.9 m, thick top) and one bentwood chair; one worn leather ARMCHAIR near the stove. A wall nail beside the door holds Morozov's black frock coat (Scenes 2–4; bare nail in Scene 9).
- **Color palette (constant):** Chalk-white walls, near-black beams and floorboards, gray blankets, black piano — a monochrome cell; the only permanent color is the honey oak of the table and the brass of the kerosene LAMP on it.
- **Floor:** Wide dark-stained pine boards running east–west, gaps blackened; a small braided oval rug (faded blue-gray) beside the bed.
- **Ceiling:** Whitewashed plaster between five exposed beams.
- **Windows and light:** Day scenes: single soft north-European daylight through the west casement, 5600 K, falling across the table. Night scenes: the brass kerosene table lamp (2000 K, 1 m pool of light) and/or a single candle in a brass holder; stove-door glow (Scenes 2, 3) as a deep-orange fill from the north wall.
- **Practical elements:** The window panel (opens ~20 times across the film — its squeak-and-clack is a recurring sound motif); the piano; the stove door; the kerosene lamp's wick key; the casement latch; the main door lock (rust-sealed by Scene 6, grinding open).

### State A — Scene 2, Year One (1886)
Room clean and spare. On the table: neat stack of yellow-backed French novels (12 volumes), inkstand, water carafe. Piano OPEN, sheet music on the stand (Chopin). Fire lit. Frock coat on its nail, brushed. Bed made square. Mood: monastic hope.

### State B — Scene 3, Year Five (1890)
Piano SHUT, a visible gray dust film on the lid. Along the east wall, 34 empty dark-green wine bottles in a rank. Table littered: dirty plates, bread crusts, overturned inkwell with a dried black stain in the wood grain (this stain remains in all later states — continuity), manuscript pages. Bed unmade, gray sheets. Floor unswept; by scene's end, covered in torn paper scraps. One bottle standing on the seat of a chair beside the bed. Mood: rot in monochrome.

### State C — Scene 4, Years Six–Ten (1891–94)
Bottles gone. BOOKS everywhere with obsessive order: towers of 40–60 volumes each on the piano lid, along the east and north walls (600 total impression), open dictionaries ranked on the table, a hand-drawn map of the ancient world pinned to the whitewash above the table. For the final Gospel beat (winter 1894): all towers gone, walls bare, one small plain wooden cross (20 cm, unvarnished) nailed where the map was, a single black-bound New Testament centered on the otherwise empty table. Mood: campaign headquarters, then hermit's cell.

### State D — Scenes 7 and 9, The Last Night (1900)
The hermit's cell fifteen years deep: everything of State C's final beat plus decay — whitewash flaking in continents near the ceiling, beams cobwebbed at the corners, the stove cold. On the table: one guttering candle in the brass holder (burned to 3 cm in Scene 7; a solidified wax puddle by Scene 9), the LETTER (one page, fine black script), and one closed book. The chair holds the sleeping Morozov (Scene 7) / stands pushed back at an angle (Scene 9). Scene 9 additions: casement window wide open, its white muslin curtain hanging out over the sill, rain-soaked; wet leaf fragments on the sill and floor beneath; gray morning light replacing the candle. Mood: a tomb the occupant has out-lived.

## LOCATION 3 — VOLKOV MANSION, STUDY (Scenes 5, 8, 9)

- **Architecture and dimensions:** 6 m × 5 m × 3.5 m, on the mansion's garden side. One tall window (south wall, 2.5 m, heavy bottle-green velvet curtains open) looking across the black garden to the lodge's lit window — the reverse of the drawing room sightline. Door in the north wall.
- **Color palette:** Dark bottle-green painted walls above walnut wainscoting; oxblood leather; dull brass; ash-gray cold fireplace. The 1900 palette is the 1885 drawing-room palette drained of its gold.
- **Floor:** Dark oak boards; a worn Turkmen rug (rust-red, geometric) under the desk.
- **Ceiling:** Smoke-darkened plaster, plain cornice.
- **Furniture and placement:** Center-east, the vast walnut partners' DESK (2 m), its top a chaos of ledgers, loose share certificates, bank notices on blue paper, a pencil, an abacus, a green-shaded brass kerosene lamp (the room's key light). Behind it an oxblood leather desk chair. West wall: the cold white-marble FIREPLACE, and above it the 1885 PORTRAIT of Volkov from the drawing room — ruddy, laughing, arms crossed — now soot-dimmed. South-west corner: the black iron FIREPROOF SAFE, 1.2 m tall, double dial, maker's plate in brass ("Санъ-Галли"). North wall: glass-front bookcase (law and finance volumes, uniform bindings) and an oxblood leather DAYBED with a folded plaid rug. Mantel clock: black marble, reading 3:02 in Scene 5, 6:15 in Scene 8.
- **Walls:** Besides the portrait: framed maps of railway concessions, an 1880s photograph of the Moscow exchange — a museum of the lost fortune.
- **Windows and light:** Scene 5/8 night: the green-shaded desk lamp (1900 K through green glass — sickly gold-green pool) plus rain-streaked blue-black window; Scene 8 end: slate-gray pre-dawn creeping in. Scene 9: flat gray morning light, lamp off.
- **Practical elements:** The safe (combination dials, cracking wax seal on the key envelope, the final triple-clank lock); the desk papers; the window (Volkov's watching-post toward the lodge); the daybed; the study door (hammered on from outside in Scene 8).
- **Ambient mood:** Cold, indebted, sleepless; rain against glass as the constant sound.
- **Time of day:** Scene 5: 3 a.m. Scene 8: 3:30 a.m. → 6:15 a.m. Scene 9 (study portion): ~8 a.m.

## LOCATION 4 — ESTATE GARDEN (Scenes 4 exterior beat, 6, 9)

- **Environment:** Ten hectares of formal garden gone half-wild between mansion and lodge, 300 m apart on a straight gravel path. Rows of bare lindens and oaks (November: black wet trunks, drifts of dead brown leaves shin-deep across the path). Along the path, four white marble STATUES on plinths, lichen-stained — the second from the mansion is a Diana with a broken bow arm (the shape Volkov mistakes for a person in Scene 6). Midway, the walled KITCHEN GARDEN (2 m brick wall, wooden gate) with STEPAN'S HUT built against it: a one-room log hut, small orange-lit window, sheepskin-covered plank bed visible through the door crack. The LODGE sits at the garden's far south end; beyond it, the estate WALL and iron GATE opening to the road.
- **Color palette:** Night (Scene 6): blue-black, wet charcoal, the lamp's amber sphere 3 m across, cold blue-white where lightning-less storm light shows the sky. Day (Scenes 4, 9): drab gray sky, wet brown leaf-carpet, black branches, white statue-marble, gray mist at 100 m.
- **Light sources:** Scene 6: Volkov's hurricane lamp (sole warm source), the lodge's candle-lit window ahead, the hut's ember-orange window. Scene 9: overcast dawn, 6500 K, shadowless.
- **Weather:** Scene 6: driving cold rain, gusting wind, trees heaving, "a keen damp wind howling in the garden and giving the trees no rest." Scene 9: rain stopped, everything soaked and dripping, standing puddles, low mist.
- **Practical elements:** The gravel path (crunching leaves — Volkov's footsteps are a dread-metronome in Scene 6); the Diana statue; the hut door; the lodge porch, its main window (tap-tap on the glass) and rusted door lock; in Scene 9 the lodge's open casement with the soaked curtain, the leaf-trail of footprints from window to gate, the open iron gate.

## LOCATION 5 — ESTATE GATE AND ROAD (Scene 9, final shot)

- **Environment:** The estate's iron double gate, 3 m, standing open, hinges rust-streaked, flanked by stuccoed brick pillars capped with stone urns. Beyond: a straight unpaved dirt road, puddled wheel-ruts, running due south between flat bare stubble-fields to a horizon 2 km off. No figure, no vehicle, no bird — deliberately, absolutely empty.
- **Color palette:** Gray sky (60% of frame), gray-brown road, ochre-brown stubble, black wet gateposts. Desaturated to near-monochrome; the one saturated note is the rust bleeding down the gate pillars.
- **Light:** Overcast morning, 6500 K, flat and shadowless; wind visibly shivering the puddles.
- **Practical elements:** The open gate itself — the film's last image of the threshold Morozov finally crossed.
