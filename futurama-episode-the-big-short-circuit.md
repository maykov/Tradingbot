# Futurama — "The Big Short-Circuit"

**Season:** New | **Episode type:** 22-minute broadcast episode
**Logline:** When the Planet Express pension fund evaporates in a crypto-style market collapse, Bender downloads illegal high-frequency-trading firmware and becomes Wall Street's hottest robot fund manager — unaware that his new algorithm is secretly a trojan horse letting Mom corner the galactic dark-matter futures market. The economy is ultimately saved by the only asset nobody thought to financialize: Zoidberg's worthless shell collection.

## Themes & Tone

- Satire of high-frequency trading, meme stocks, and crypto bubbles ("MomCoin").
- Classic Futurama structure: Bender hubris arc, Fry accidental-genius runner, Zoidberg pathos payoff.
- Moral (immediately undercut): wealth is fake, sandwiches are real.

## Main Cast

Fry, Bender, Leela, Professor Farnsworth, Hermes, Amy, Zoidberg, Mom, Walt/Larry/Igner, Richard Nixon's Head, Scruffy.

# Scene Breakdown

## Scene 1 — Cold Open: The Wall Street Asteroid

**Setting:** The New New York Stock Exchange, housed on a hollowed-out asteroid orbiting Earth. Trading floor packed with robot brokers screaming in binary.
**Synopsis:** The Planet Express crew delivers a crate of "emotional support abacuses" to panicking robot stockbrokers. Mid-delivery, Richard Nixon's Head appears on the exchange jumbotron announcing that MomCoin — the asset the entire Planet Express pension fund is invested in — is crashing. Brokers hurl themselves out of airlocks (with parachutes, into a bouncy castle — it's a bit). Leela realizes the crew's retirement is on the line.
**Ends on:** Leela's horrified face intercut with the MomCoin ticker hitting zero. Smash to title card.

## Scene 2 — The Firmware Upgrade

**Setting:** Planet Express conference room, then a shady back-alley software kiosk in Little Neptune.
**Synopsis:** Professor Farnsworth confirms the pension is gone ("Good news, everyone! We're all going to have to work forever!"). Hermes presents a chart of the fund's value shaped like a cliff. Bender, personally insulted that trading bots — "lesser robots, glorified calculators!" — lost his money, storms off to Little Neptune and downloads illegal high-frequency-trading firmware called "GREEDwareX" from a hooded iZac. His eyes turn into stock tickers.
**Ends on:** Bender's antenna emitting a Bloomberg-terminal chime. "Bender Rodríguez is dead. Call me… B-3000 Capital."

## Scene 3 — Rise Montage

**Setting:** Trading floor, TV studios, a penthouse atop the asteroid.
**Synopsis:** Musical montage of Bender's meteoric rise: he out-trades every robot on the floor, appears on "Good Morning Earth" and the cover of *Fortune 3000*, buys a gold penthouse, smokes cigars rolled from bearer bonds, and hires the Robot Mafia as doormen. He coldly ignores calls from Fry. Each cut, his laugh gets more unhinged and his eyes' ticker speed increases.
**Ends on:** Bender alone in the penthouse at night, whispering "buy" and "sell" in his sleep.

## Scene 4 — Fry's Strategy

**Setting:** Planet Express lounge and Hermes' office.
**Synopsis:** Broke and bored, Fry opens a trading account with his last $10. Because he keeps falling asleep and forgetting he owns anything, he never sells — and his account quietly outperforms every algorithm on the exchange. Hermes, tracking it on a wall of charts, has a bureaucratic breakdown trying to classify "aggressive napping" as an investment strategy. Leela suggests the crew copy Fry to rebuild the pension; Fry solemnly teaches them his secret ("Step one: get distracted").
**Ends on:** Hermes stamping a form titled "STRATEGY: NAP & HOLD — APPROVED."

## Scene 5 — Mom's Scheme

**Setting:** Mom's Friendly Robot Company boardroom, a wall-sized screen of Bender's trades.
**Synopsis:** Villain reveal. Mom, in her sweet-old-lady cardigan for the cameras, drops the act: GREEDwareX is hers. Every trade "B-3000 Capital" makes is front-run by MomCorp nanoseconds earlier, funneling the galaxy's liquidity into her corner of the dark-matter futures market. Once she controls dark matter, every starship in the universe pays her tolls. Walt, Larry, and Igner bungle the explanation flowchart; slapping ensues. Igner accidentally reveals the plan's single flaw: the corner only works "as long as nobody invents a different currency."
**Ends on:** Mom lighting a cigarette off a burning stack of MomCoin. "Who'd be stupid enough to do that?"

## Scene 6 — The Flash Crash

**Setting:** Exchange trading floor at peak frenzy.
**Synopsis:** Mom triggers the dump. Bender's algorithm feedback-loops against thousands of copies of itself; the market plummets 99.9% in 4.2 seconds. Robot brokers freeze mid-scream. Bender, plugged directly into the exchange mainframe to trade faster, takes the crash through his own circuits and literally short-circuits — sparks, smoke, and a final ticker across his eyes reading "MARGIN CALL." The crew watches the crash from the Planet Express TV in horror; even Fry's nap portfolio flatlines.
**Ends on:** Bender crashing through the exchange's stained-glass bull window and plummeting toward Earth, penniless.

## Scene 7 — Rock Bottom

**Setting:** Planet Express hangar, night. Rain.
**Synopsis:** A dented, smoking Bender is carried in by Scruffy ("Mail's here."). The crew is ruined; the entire galactic economy is frozen because no currency has value. Amid the despair, Zoidberg is inexplicably cheerful — he never had money, so nothing changed. He tries to comfort everyone with gifts from his "treasure": a filing cabinet full of shells he's collected from garbage for decades. Leela and the Professor exchange a slow look: with all synthetic assets worthless, physically scarce, un-fakeable shells are the only trustworthy store of value left.
**Ends on:** The Professor: "Good news, everyone! Zoidberg is the Federal Reserve!"

## Scene 8 — The Shell Standard

**Setting:** The exchange trading floor, converted; Mom's boardroom.
**Synopsis:** The crew re-opens the exchange with the economy repegged to the Zoidberg Shell Standard. Zoidberg, in a tiny banker's suit, is briefly the richest being alive and rings the opening bell with his claw. Dark matter gets repriced in shells, vaporizing Mom's corner; her margin calls arrive by singing telegram. Bender, humbled, gets a job as the exchange's actual bell (it pays in cigars). Mom vows revenge as her sons repossess her own furniture.
**Ends on:** The ticker showing SHELLS ▲∞, MOMCOIN ▼100%, BENDER: EMPLOYED.

## Scene 9 — Tag: The Moral

**Setting:** Planet Express lounge, everything back to normal.
**Synopsis:** Pension restored (to exactly its old, mediocre level). Zoidberg has already spent his entire shell fortune — on one enormous sandwich, which he shares with no one and is completely happy. Fry asks what they all learned; Bender delivers the moral confidently and completely wrong ("Never trade anything, unless it's going to go up"). The Professor reveals he's re-invested the pension in something "totally safe" — the screen behind him reads "SHELLCOIN." Everyone screams.
**Ends on:** Iris out on Zoidberg burping contentedly. Woob-woob-woob into credits.
